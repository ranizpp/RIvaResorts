Where the Ghats meet the plateau

Why this corner of Wayanad feels the way it does, and how to get here.

Why this place matters

Riva sits at the edge of the Deccan plateau, where the Western Ghats fall away toward the Kerala coast. That position shapes everything guests feel here: cool, misty mornings, green slopes and clean air.

A kilometre away, Pookode Lake feeds into the Kabini, a major tributary of the Cauvery, so the water you see here is part of the river system that crosses South India. It's an easy place for families to slow down, and for small groups to gather without crowds.

01, The setting

The plateau edge

Climb from the coast through the forested Western Ghats and the land levels out into the Wayanad plateau. Riva sits right at that edge.

02, The water

From Pookode Lake to the Cauvery

The water you see a kilometre from Riva doesn't head for the nearby Arabian Sea. It flows east, into one of South India's great river systems.

Pookode Lake

A freshwater lake ringed by forest, 1 km from Riva.

Kabini

The lake feeds into the Kabini, which flows east into Karnataka.

Cauvery

The Kabini is a major tributary of the Cauvery, one of South India's great rivers.

Bay of Bengal

The Cauvery crosses the peninsula to the east coast.

The Arabian Sea is a short drive west, yet the water here travels right across South India.
